Shapes, and the same thing for one token

With n tokens, X is n×d, each W is d×dₖ, and Q, K and V are n×dₖ. So QKᵀ is n×n and Z = AV is n×dₖ. (In general V may have a different width, dᵥ.)

For token i: zᵢ = Σⱼ αᵢⱼ vⱼ, where αᵢⱼ = exp(qᵢ·kⱼ/√dₖ) / Σₗ exp(qᵢ·kₗ/√dₖ).

Multi-head attention runs this h times with different W matrices, concatenates the outputs, and multiplies by one more learned matrix W_O.

“Gradient” is the Frobenius norm of softmax’s Jacobian with respect to its input. The median is shown because a few near-ties keep the mean up after the typical draw has saturated.

Derivation: variance and saturation

For independent entries with mean 0 and variance 1, each product qₘkₘ has mean 0 and variance E[qₘ²]E[kₘ²] = 1. The terms are uncorrelated, so Var(q·k) = dₖ and Var(q·k/√dₖ) = 1.

For p = softmax(s), the Jacobian is ∂pᵢ/∂sⱼ = pᵢ(δᵢⱼ − pⱼ). As p approaches one-hot, every entry approaches 0, so little gradient reaches Q and K.

Trained vectors aren’t independent Gaussians. The argument explains why scaling is a good starting point for training, and doesn’t claim that trained scores have variance 1.

Why does zeroing afterwards leak?

αᵢⱼ = exp(sᵢⱼ) / Σₗ exp(sᵢₗ). Zeroing future weights removes them from the numerator, but their scores are still in every denominator, so a future token rescales the past. Setting sᵢₗ = −∞ first removes them from both.

Zeroing and then renormalizing is equivalent in exact arithmetic, but can become 0/0 when a large future score underflows all the visible weights.

Where do q, k and v come from in GPT-2?

X = LayerNorm(token embedding + position embedding), 768 numbers per token. One learned matrix of shape 768×2304 (plus a bias) produces Q, K and V at once, and each is split into 12 heads of 64.

Only the first layer is shown. In deeper layers each position already mixes information from earlier tokens, so “attending to a token” no longer means attending to that word alone.
